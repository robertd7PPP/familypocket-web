About this information

Competitor details are based on publicly available information checked on 31 October 2026 and editorial review feedback identified in the app notes. The current app may differ, and features, free limits and payment options can vary by country, plan and app version or change over time. “Unclear” means a detail could not be confirmed from the available information. This comparison is published by FamilyPocket and is not a comprehensive hands-on test of every app.

01FamilyPocketTracking before kid banking+

Manual deposits and spending are unlimited and free. The first 20 job approvals or direct job payments are included across the household, cumulatively—not per child or per month. After those are used, Family Plan is needed for further job payments and automatic allowance. Manual tracking remains free. Wishlists show progress against a child’s single balance rather than separate saving pots. The current history screen offers up to 12 months; this is a viewing window, not a claim that older entries are deleted.

02Chores & Allowance BotFree basics; paid extra chore features+

Free use includes basic chores, allowances, manual deposits/spending and family-device sync. Individually paid Reward chores, up-for-grabs/rotating chores, extra accounts/goals and unlimited history are Premium. Free history is described as recent; its exact length and any numerical manual-entry cap are not specified. Children can link by code without their own email. Scheduled allowance differs from Premium’s automatic chore-dependent approval. Parent-set automatic interest was not established.

03ChorseeAutomatic chore rewards; family admin devices+

Chorsee is compared as a record-keeping tracker, with real money handled outside the app. Completing a chore can automatically credit its reward; photo review does not establish a hold-for-parent-approval option. Multiple household devices can have parent/admin access. Child mode documents a personal chore list and editing restrictions, but balance visibility and child-email requirements remain unclear. Date-filtered earnings and adjustments are documented; the maximum history window is not stated. Current free caps and the purchase option applicable to two children are also unclear. 05BomadFive free transactions; no job flow+

Bomad records money a parent owes a child and can track external balances, with real money handled outside the app. The free limit is five transactions in total across all transaction types, then payment is required. This is one combined limit, not five per type. There is no built-in job assignment, claim or approval flow. These two details were supplied during editorial review. Provider store descriptions document separate spending, saving and giving accounts, allowance splits and transaction history; their free entitlement and maximum history window are not established. 06NatWest Rooster MoneyUK free tracker with a paid card plan+

What Low, Medium and High mean

These are indicative costs for a two-child family using the ordinary local entry plan or usable free tracker. One-off purchases and subscriptions are shown separately. The bands describe cost, not quality, and exclude temporary promotions, hardware and extra transaction/card fees. A free tracker can have paid extras; eligibility-based offers have their own conditions. “Unclear” is used where a dependable comparison could not be made.

For example, FamilyPocket’s manual tracking is free, while Family Plan keeps jobs and automatic allowances running after its 20 included household job payments. Rooster’s free tracker and its paid card offer are different modes. Check the provider’s current local plan before choosing.

Why these apps?

What was checked?

Provider product pages, help documents, plan information and store listings were used to check purpose, features and free limits. Ratings and downloads helped discover candidates; they were not used as a quality score. Unresolved or conflicting details remain marked Unclear. Details supplied during editorial review are identified in the app notes.

Card and banking services have their own residency, age and parent-account rules. A tracker being listed in an app store does not prove every service is available in every country. For this comparison, the Tracking Apps are treated as record-keeping tools that do not hold or transfer money or provide child spending cards.
